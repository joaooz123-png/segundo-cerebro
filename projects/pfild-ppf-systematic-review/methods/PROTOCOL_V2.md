# Protocol v2 — Systematic evidence census and nested systematic reviews of PF-ILD / PPF

**Protocol version:** 2.0  
**Protocol freeze point:** before human title/abstract screening, eligibility decisions, analytical extraction, risk-of-bias assessment or synthesis  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-03  
**Status:** operational protocol awaiting named-author approval and time-stamped external registration  

## 1. Transparency statement on protocol timing

This version is not represented as having been written before all review activity. Before protocol v2 was frozen, the team had already:

- developed and tested search terminology;
- executed formal or provisional searches in PubMed/MEDLINE, Europe PMC, ClinicalTrials.gov and selected other open sources;
- enriched bibliographic metadata;
- created non-decisional machine-priority queues;
- tested exact deduplication and publication-family linkage;
- performed citation chasing from a sentinel set.

At the protocol-freeze point, the following had **not** begun:

- human title/abstract eligibility decisions;
- human full-text eligibility decisions;
- final inclusion or exclusion;
- outcome extraction for analytical synthesis;
- risk-of-bias assessment;
- meta-analysis, narrative synthesis or certainty assessment.

All earlier methodological development is preserved in version control. Any subsequent change must be entered in the amendment log with date, rationale, stage and expected effect.

## 2. Review architecture

The project has two linked but methodologically distinct products.

### 2.1 Primary product: systematic evidence census and evidence map

The primary product will identify, classify and map the breadth, chronology, terminology, etiologies, designs, document roles and research domains of literature directly addressing progressive fibrosing interstitial lung disease (PF-ILD), progressive pulmonary fibrosis (PPF) or an equivalent progressive fibrotic ILD phenotype.

This product will be reported using PRISMA 2020, PRISMA-S and PRISMA-ScR principles as applicable. It is not intended to calculate a single pooled effect across heterogeneous document types.

### 2.2 Nested analytical systematic reviews

Within the mapped corpus, separate systematic reviews may be conducted for clinically coherent questions. Each nested review will have a specific PICO/PECO or other question framework, eligible designs, outcomes, risk-of-bias tool and synthesis plan. No study will be pooled merely because it appears in the evidence census.

Planned analytical domains include:

1. definitions, diagnostic criteria and classification;
2. incidence, prevalence and burden;
3. natural history, progression and mortality;
4. prognostic factors and biomarkers;
5. imaging and quantitative imaging;
6. pulmonary-function and patient-reported monitoring;
7. antifibrotic interventions;
8. immunomodulatory and etiology-specific interventions;
9. non-pharmacological, supportive and palliative care;
10. health economics, resource use and patient journey;
11. treatment safety;
12. etiology-specific PPF populations.

A domain will proceed to analytical synthesis only after its question and methods have been frozen in a domain-specific appendix or protocol amendment before outcome extraction.

## 3. Review objectives

### 3.1 Primary objective

To create a reproducible and updateable census of records and study families that directly address PF-ILD/PPF or an equivalent progressive fibrotic ILD phenotype.

### 3.2 Secondary objectives

- describe changes in terminology and operational definitions over time;
- distinguish bibliographic records, reports, studies and study families;
- map evidence by underlying ILD, population, geography, design, document type and research domain;
- identify unpublished, ongoing, preprint, conference and regulatory evidence;
- identify gaps and redundant research;
- create analytically coherent subsets for nested systematic reviews;
- evaluate risk of bias and certainty only within appropriate analytical questions.

## 4. Conceptual scope and terminology

### 4.1 Target construct

The target construct is progression of a fibrosing interstitial lung disease other than idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), whether described using modern PPF terminology, earlier PF-ILD terminology or an explicitly equivalent phenotype.

### 4.2 Terminological eras

Records will be tagged rather than excluded solely because terminology changed.

- **Modern PPF era:** records using PPF or criteria aligned with the 2022 ATS/ERS/JRS/ALAT concept.
- **PF-ILD development era:** records using PF-ILD, progressive fibrosing ILD, progressive fibrotic phenotype or trial-specific progression criteria.
- **Historical-equivalent era:** records predating the standardized labels but explicitly studying progressive fibrotic behavior across non-IPF ILDs.

Modern diagnostic terminology will not be retroactively required for older studies when the investigated phenotype is substantively equivalent.

## 5. Units of management

The project will maintain four distinct units.

1. **Retrieval unit:** an item returned by a source or export.
2. **Bibliographic record:** a normalized representation of one identifiable document.
3. **Report:** a distinct document communicating information, such as an article, abstract, protocol, letter, correction, thesis or regulatory review.
4. **Study/family:** the underlying investigation or related program that may produce multiple reports and registry records.

Multiple reports from one study will not be treated as duplicate reports. Multiple source representations of the same report may be collapsed while preserving source provenance.

## 6. Eligibility for the evidence census

Eligibility will be judged at report level. Inclusion in the census does not imply eligibility for a nested analytical synthesis.

### 6.1 Direct target-evidence layer

A report is eligible for the direct target-evidence layer when it satisfies at least one of the following:

1. PF-ILD or PPF is a primary population, condition, phenotype, exposure, intervention indication, outcome, classification target or explicit central concept;
2. the report develops, applies, compares or validates progression criteria for fibrosing non-IPF ILD;
3. the report presents separable data for patients with a progressive fibrosing non-IPF ILD phenotype;
4. the report evaluates an intervention specifically in PF-ILD/PPF or a clearly equivalent population;
5. the report is a protocol, registry entry, conference abstract, correction, correspondence, regulatory document or other report directly linked to an eligible study or central PF-ILD/PPF question.

### 6.2 Contextual layer

A report may enter a separately labelled contextual layer when it is not direct target evidence but is necessary to interpret the origin, measurement or application of the construct. Examples include:

- foundational non-IPF fibrotic ILD classification or prognostic frameworks repeatedly used to define progression;
- IPF-derived methods or outcomes explicitly transferred to PF-ILD/PPF;
- regulatory or guideline documents defining scope boundaries;
- methodological papers on progression measurement that directly informed PF-ILD/PPF criteria.

Contextual reports will not be counted as direct target evidence and will not enter effect estimates unless they independently meet a nested review’s criteria.

### 6.3 Population

Eligible populations include humans of any age with a fibrosing ILD other than IPF that is progressive, suspected to be progressive or being evaluated for progression. Examples include, but are not limited to:

- connective-tissue-disease-associated ILD;
- rheumatoid-arthritis-associated ILD;
- systemic-sclerosis-associated ILD;
- fibrotic hypersensitivity pneumonitis;
- idiopathic nonspecific interstitial pneumonia;
- unclassifiable ILD;
- fibrotic sarcoidosis;
- occupational and exposure-related fibrotic ILD;
- fibrotic ILD associated with myositis or other systemic autoimmune disease;
- pediatric progressive fibrosing ILD when explicitly relevant.

### 6.4 Mixed populations

Mixed IPF/non-IPF or stable/progressive cohorts are eligible for the direct layer when:

- PF-ILD/PPF is explicitly part of the research question; or
- data for the target phenotype are separately extractable; or
- the report is a study-family document for an eligible PF-ILD/PPF investigation.

The separability of target data will be recorded. Mixed data that cannot support a nested quantitative analysis may remain eligible for mapping.

### 6.5 IPF-only reports

IPF-only reports are not direct PF-ILD/PPF evidence. They may be retained in the contextual layer only when the report has an explicit, material connection to the PF-ILD/PPF construct, such as transferred criteria, outcome validation, mechanism, trial rationale or regulatory comparison. Mere mention of progressive fibrosis or generic antifibrotic treatment is insufficient.

### 6.6 Mechanistic and non-human evidence

Animal, in-vitro and translational mechanistic reports are excluded from the human direct-evidence layer. They may be mapped in a separate mechanistic contextual module only when they explicitly model, explain or test the PF-ILD/PPF construct or an intervention developed for it. They will never be combined with human clinical estimates.

### 6.7 Document types

No document type is excluded from the evidence census solely because of format. Eligible formats may include:

- randomized and non-randomized trials;
- cohort, case-control, cross-sectional and diagnostic/prognostic studies;
- case series where the target phenotype is central;
- systematic, scoping and narrative reviews;
- guidelines, consensus statements and position papers;
- protocols and registry entries;
- conference abstracts and proceedings;
- preprints;
- theses and dissertations;
- books and chapters;
- editorials, letters, replies and corrections;
- health-technology assessments and regulatory documents;
- qualitative and mixed-methods studies;
- economic evaluations.

Each report will be assigned a document role. Opinion documents will be mapped but will not be treated as primary effect evidence.

### 6.8 Exclusion from both direct and contextual layers

Exclude when the report:

- uses PPF for an unrelated meaning;
- concerns pulmonary fibrosis without an interstitial-lung-disease progression construct relevant to the review;
- is exclusively IPF without a material PF-ILD/PPF connection;
- mentions PF-ILD/PPF only incidentally and contributes no target-specific information;
- is a duplicate source representation of the same report after exact verification;
- cannot be identified sufficiently to establish that a report exists;
- is a news item, commercial page or non-scholarly material without evidentiary or regulatory value.

## 7. Restrictions

- no date restriction;
- no language restriction;
- no geographic restriction;
- no publication-status restriction;
- no outcome-reporting restriction for census eligibility.

Translation methods and any inaccessible-language limitations will be documented.

## 9. Search methods

Searches will combine controlled vocabulary, text words, acronyms, hyphenation, plural forms, historical terms, progression language and etiology-specific context where required by the platform.

The PubMed strategy is the primary strategy for PRESS peer review. Translation to other databases will preserve concepts rather than copy syntax mechanically.

No methodological filter will be used in the main evidence-census search. No language or date limits will be applied.

Known relevant references and registry records form a sentinel set. Failure to retrieve a verified eligible sentinel must be explained and corrected or explicitly justified.

## 10. Record management and deduplication

### 10.1 Exact source-representation duplicates

The same report retrieved from multiple sources may be collapsed when identity is established using a safe combination such as:

- exact PMID;
- exact source identifier;
- DOI together with concordant title and report characteristics;
- exact title, authorship and bibliographic data after review.

Source provenance will be retained.

### 10.2 Prohibited automatic merges

The following must not be merged automatically:

- records sharing only a DOI;
- preprint and journal publication;
- protocol and results report;
- conference abstract and full article;
- letter and reply;
- correction and corrected article;
- country-specific registry entries of a multinational protocol;
- secondary analysis and primary report.

### 10.3 Study-family linkage

Family relationships will be proposed using registry identifiers, protocol numbers, acronym, sponsor, intervention, recruitment dates, sample size, authorship, centres and explicit statements. Final family linkage requires human confirmation.

## 11. Selection process

### 11.1 Machine assistance

Automated methods may:

- order records for review;
- identify exact identifiers and candidate duplicates;
- suggest publication-family links;
- flag likely meanings of PPF;
- highlight target terminology;
- detect contradictions or missing metadata.

Automated methods must not make final inclusion or exclusion decisions.

### 11.2 Human title/abstract screening

Two reviewers will independently assess records. Before full screening, a pilot set will be used to refine operational understanding without changing the frozen eligibility principles. Pilot changes must be logged.

Allowed decisions:

- include for full-text/direct layer;
- include for contextual layer;
- exclude with reason;
- uncertain.

### 11.3 Full-text eligibility

Final full-text decisions will be made independently by two reviewers. Disagreements will be resolved by consensus or a third adjudicator. Specific exclusion reasons will be recorded at report level.

### 11.4 Non-retrieval

Legal routes for obtaining reports include open access, institutional access, accepted manuscripts, repositories, interlibrary loan and author contact. Failed retrieval attempts and dates will be recorded. No paywall bypass or unauthorized source will be used.

## 12. Data charting and extraction

### 12.1 Census-level fields

- record and report identifiers;
- complete authorship;
- title, source and year;
- language and country;
- document type and role;
- terminology used;
- underlying ILD(s);
- population and setting;
- study design;
- progression definition;
- intervention/exposure and comparator where applicable;
- outcomes/domains studied;
- registry and protocol identifiers;
- publication-family links;
- access and publication status;
- direct versus contextual layer;
- domain assignment.

### 12.2 Analytical extraction

Analytical fields will be defined in domain-specific forms before outcome extraction. Data will be extracted by one reviewer and verified by another, or independently duplicated for critical variables where feasible. Discrepancies will be resolved transparently.

## 13. Risk of bias and appraisal

Risk-of-bias appraisal is not used to exclude reports from the descriptive evidence census solely on quality grounds. It will be applied within analytical questions using design-appropriate tools, potentially including:

- RoB 2 for randomized trials;
- ROBINS-I for non-randomized intervention studies;
- QUIPS for prognostic-factor studies;
- QUADAS-3 or the current appropriate diagnostic-accuracy tool;
- appropriate JBI, CASP or equivalent tools for prevalence, qualitative and other designs;
- ROBIS for systematic reviews when reviews themselves are analyzed.

Two reviewers will complete or verify critical judgments. Domain-level support for each judgment will be preserved.

## 14. Synthesis

### 14.1 Evidence map

The primary synthesis will be descriptive and structured. It may include counts, timelines, matrices, network/family maps and evidence-gap maps. Counts will distinguish records, reports and studies/families.

### 14.2 Nested quantitative synthesis

Meta-analysis will be performed only where populations, definitions, interventions/exposures, comparators, outcomes and designs are sufficiently coherent. Models, effect measures, time points, heterogeneity evaluation, sensitivity analyses and handling of multiplicity will be specified before extraction for each analytical domain.

### 14.3 Synthesis without meta-analysis

Where meta-analysis is not appropriate, structured synthesis will follow SWiM principles where applicable. Vote counting based only on statistical significance will not be used.

## 15. Certainty of evidence

GRADE will be used for appropriate intervention and other effect questions. GRADE-CERQual may be used for qualitative findings when applicable. Certainty will not be assigned to the entire heterogeneous evidence census as a single rating.

## 16. Reporting biases and missing evidence

The project will compare registry records, protocols, conference abstracts, preprints and publications to identify non-publication, delayed publication, selective reporting and outcome discrepancies. Small-study and publication-bias analyses will be undertaken only when methodologically appropriate.

## 17. Updating

The review is designed as an updateable evidence system. Search updates will preserve:

- previous frozen datasets;
- exact update dates and strategies;
- new-versus-existing provenance;
- deduplication decisions;
- amendments and software versions.

An update will never overwrite the historical PRISMA flow.

## 18. Roles and independence

Named human contributors must be assigned before external registration, including:

- guarantor/lead author;
- clinical domain lead;
- information specialist/PRESS reviewer;
- two independent screeners;
- adjudicator;
- statistical/methodological lead;
- risk-of-bias reviewers.

The PRESS reviewer should be independent of the person or system that authored the final strategy. Final ROBIS/AMSTAR 2 audit should be performed by reviewers who did not solely construct the methods being audited.

## 19. Registration and dissemination

The umbrella evidence-census protocol is suitable for time-stamped registration using the OSF Generalized Systematic Review registration structure, with transparent disclosure that search development preceded registration but human selection and analysis had not begun.

Nested human-health systematic reviews that meet PROSPERO scope should be registered separately before domain-specific screening or extraction, when feasible.

Results will be disseminated through a peer-reviewed evidence map and domain-specific systematic reviews, with data and code shared to the extent permitted by source licences.

## 20. Deviations and amendments

Every protocol change after freeze must record:

- amendment ID and date;
- original and revised wording;
- reason;
- evidence or event prompting the change;
- review stage at the time;
- whether eligibility decisions had begun;
- expected direction of bias, if any;
- approving human author(s).

Silent retrospective changes are prohibited.
